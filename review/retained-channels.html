Retained channels,
and where remoteness lives

The porta protocol rests on a kind of channel the node does not have. This is what that kind is, why the protocol needs it, and the one decision it forces: what belongs in C, and what belongs in nsl.

what exists today

The space is three verbs

A job never touches the space directly. It holds a Channel capability — an object rooted at a path prefix — and everything it can do is these three sends.

(* nodus/nsl/lib/kernel.ns — the whole surface *)
public out: rel value: v  = ( platform spacePost:   (self resolve: rel) value: v )
public take: rel          = ( platform spaceTake:   (self resolve: rel) )
public whenever: rel do: b = ( platform spaceRegister: (self resolve: rel) block: b owner: self )

Underneath, in C, they are exactly what they look like: space_post() appends to a queue, space_take() pops from it, spaceRegister parks a reaction on a path. A channel today is a queue. That single fact is the whole problem.

the observation

Two things we call “state” do not behave alike

“the node rebooted”

Happened once, at a moment. Reading it consumes it. Replaying it later would be a lie — and worse, acting on it twice would reboot the node twice.

“the node’s mode is deep-sleep”

Is not an occurrence. It is simply true, and stays true until changed. A job that asks tomorrow deserves the same answer. Reading it must not destroy it.

A queue models the first perfectly and the second not at all. Every awkward rule in porta v1 — “last write wins”, “absent never clobbers”, “the command is the whole policy, not a patch”, the entire self-heal loop — is v1 hand-building the second behaviour on top of the first, one special case at a time.

channel kind 01

Event — a queue of occurrences

publisher
channel state
late reader

The value exists only in flight. take: consumes it; a reader who arrives after the fact gets nothing, and that is correct — it genuinely missed the event. Exactly-once is the contract, and the cursor is what enforces it across a reboot.

tel/print · tel/log · sys/panic · sys/reset · goal/do/reboot · dbg/req · dbg/resp

channel kind 02

Retained — a latched value

publisher
channel state
late reader

The path holds its latest value. Publishing overwrites; reading does not consume. A reader who arrives late — a job that just started, a gateway resyncing, a node that just rebooted — still gets the current truth. Deleting leaves a tombstone, because “no longer set” is itself a fact that has to sync.

goal/mode · goal/apps/** · goal/config/** · obs/health · obs/apps/**

the difference, made concrete

Ask both channels the same question, late

A supervisor restarts a crashed job. The job asks the space what its config is. What it gets back depends entirely on the channel kind — and this is the whole reason retained has to exist.

if goal/mode were an event

(* published 40 minutes ago *)
chan take: 'mode'
→ nil

The value was consumed by whoever read it first. The restarted job has no way to learn its own configuration short of asking porta over the radio — on every crash.

goal/mode as retained

(* published 40 minutes ago *)
chan peek: 'mode'
→ Mode { mode: #deepSleep, … }

Still there. The job recovers locally, at memory speed, with no network involved. Cold boot and 40th restart take the identical path.

— waiting —
why it earns its keep

Retained paths make porta’s special cases dissolve

porta v1 rule (bespoke, prose-enforced)what it is once paths are retained
“commands are declarative and absolute, last write wins”…the definition of a retained path
set-mode must be accepted whole or rejected whole…one record at one path. Atomic by construction — nothing to enforce
set-forward “is the whole policy, not a patch”…a retained value simply is the whole value
config echo “only on cold boot and on change; absent never clobbers”…publish-on-change. There is no cache to clobber
gateway self-heal: diff desired vs reported, re-enqueue the divergent…a tree diff of goal/ against obs/. The mechanism disappears
reboot’s exception paragraph (imperative, exactly-once, terminal)…an event channel. The exception was the other kind, all along

v1's unit of extension is the verb, and verbs multiply: each new piece of node state touches the codec, the report shape, the echo rules, self-heal, and every node kind. A space's unit of extension is the path, and paths just add.

local → remote

The same verbs, across a wire

Porta mirrors the node's space. Sync is a cursor: “give me everything with seq > what I last saw.” That one mechanism carries both channel kinds, and it is why a retained path needs a per-path sequence number, not just a value.

porta                                   node
  node/<id>/goal/**   ─── pull ──▶   goal/**   retained in
  node/<id>/obs/**    ◀── push ───   obs/**    retained out
  node/<id>/tel/**    ◀── push ───   tel/**    events out
  node/<id>/dbg/req   ─── pull ──▶   dbg/req   events in

Cold boot is not a special case. The cursor resets to 0, porta replays the whole retained tree, and the node converges. v1 needed boot-echo drift-healing rules to get here; retained paths get it by construction.

the one law

take: never crosses the wire

Location transparency must not become failure transparency.
Waldo, Wyant, Wollrath & Kendall — A Note on Distributed Computing, Sun Labs TR-94-29

A destructive read across a network is a consensus problem — it is exactly where JavaSpaces was forced to grow transactions. So the wire does not offer one. Neither side destructively reads the other's space; each publishes into its own, and the other syncs.

what crosses

out: — best-effort, batched, eventually delivered.
whenever: — a subscription: changes since a cursor.

what does not

take: — never. Not restricted, not discouraged. Absent. There is no wire form of it to misuse.

The wire makes remote nameable, not invisible. Every delivery contract stays explicit, and none of them lie.

the mechanism you already built

A capability is a rooted path

This is the part that makes the rest work, and it is already shipped. A Channel is an object holding a root prefix. Every verb resolves through it, so a job cannot name a path outside its root — not by policy, but because it has no way to express one.

(* kernel.ns — attenuation: a capability can only narrow, never widen *)
resolve: rel = (
  outer checkPath: rel.               (* crash on any escape attempt *)
  rel = '' ifTrue: [ ^ root ].
  root = '' ifTrue: [ ^ rel ].
  ^ root , '/' , rel )                (* explicit '/' — 'a/b' can never collide with 'a/bc' *)

public channel: rel = ( ^ Channel new setRoot: (self resolve: rel) )

Hand a job chan channel: 'goal' and it holds authority over goal/** and nothing else — forever, transitively, with no ambient authority to fall back on. Capabilities are constructor arguments (the Milestone-C rule), so a job's reach is fixed at the moment it is born.

the thesis

Remoteness lives in the grant

The bridge is an ordinary supervised job. It is special only in what it was handed: the radio capability, plus write access to the inbound prefixes and read access to the outbound ones. It is the single door — so overflow policy, auth, and version skew are enforced in exactly one place, and nowhere else has to know porta exists.

the tension

The design contradicts itself, and it matters

PROTOCOL-NSL §2

“Two channel kinds, declared per path prefix … retained — the path holds a latest value; sync transfers values changed since a cursor; a delete is a tombstone.”

→ retained is a property of the space

PROTOCOL-NSL §3.1

“An ordinary supervised nsl job, granted … No VM changes; crash of the bridge is a contained fault.”

→ retained is somebody's private bookkeeping

Both cannot hold. The space in C today has no latest-value slot, no per-path sequence, no tombstone and no “changed since cursor” query. Something has to provide those four things, and which thing decides who else can use them.

the fork

Four things must exist. Where?

neededin nsl — a library over the event spacein C — a real second channel kind
latest value per patha Map in the bridge joba slot on the channel
per-path seqan int in that Mapa counter on the channel
tombstonea sentinel in that Mapa flag on the channel
changedSince: cursora scan of that Mapa new prim
costzero new C. Reversible. Heap-visible, counts against the 48 KB arenanew prims, new GC roots, footprint — and it reopens the VM after 8 rungs of not touching it
who can use itonly the bridge. Any other job must go through the bridge's private APIevery job, through the capability it already holds

The last row is not a detail. It is the whole decision.

the question that decides it

Who, other than the bridge, needs a retained path?

If the answer is nobody — if retained state is purely the bridge's sync bookkeeping — then an nsl library is sufficient, cheap, and reversible, and §3.1's “no VM changes” is honest.

But the design says otherwise, twice:

  • §3.2: “the supervisor reacts to goal/apps/** exactly as it reacts to any channel.”
  • §3.3: “the protocol is the space, filtered by grant — there is no separate protocol handler.”

Both of those claims are only true if retained is real space semantics. Put it in the bridge's private Map, and the supervisor must ask the bridge — which means jobs now know the bridge exists, the bridge becomes a second kind of authority alongside the space, and “the protocol is the space” quietly becomes false.

Either retained is a channel kind and the space owns it — or the bridge owns it and the elegance of §1 is the first casualty. That is the trade, stated honestly. It is a design call, not a cost call.

necessary vs. better

What is forced, and what is taste

genuinely forced into C

Nothing yet — if only the bridge reads retained paths. An nsl Map can hold a value, an int, and a tombstone. There is no VM capability being asked for here that nsl lacks.

forced into C the moment…

…a second job needs a retained path. Then the space must own it, because the space is the only thing every job already has a capability into.

So the honest framing is not “C is faster” or “nsl is cheaper”. It is: an nsl library is a bet that the bridge is the only consumer, forever. A C channel kind is the bet that it is not — paid up front, in prims and footprint.

There is a third path worth naming: build it in nsl first, host-only, on the G10 fleet-sim. It is throwaway-cheap, it will tell us what the Map actually costs against a 48 KB arena, and the moment a second consumer appears, we will have learned exactly what the C version must expose. The prototype is the argument.

where this leaves us

Decided, and open

settled

The retained/event split, and which paths are which. The cursor as the one sync mechanism. Tombstones. take: never crossing the wire. Remoteness enforced at the grant, in one door. The record schemas and the size law that keeps bulk data off the channels entirely.

open — and yours

Does the space own retained, or does the bridge? Everything downstream — the bridge job, porta's store, the fleet-sim — is straightforward once this is answered. Nothing downstream is safe to build before it.
