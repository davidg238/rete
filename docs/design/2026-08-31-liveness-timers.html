Every Clock in the Liveness Path

Ten constants across three tiers — nine on the node, one on the gateway, none in custos. What each guards against, which derive from each other, and the one that derives from nothing.

§ 1

The short version

One number is declared by the node — cadence = 2000 ms. From it, two different deadlines of 16 000 ms are computed independently on opposite sides of the wire, by two multiplications by 8 that were chosen separately and happen to match.

Then porta, which is the side that must act on the second one, can only re-form a verdict every 30 000 ms. Its clock is coarser than the budget it is judging against.

That is #273, in one line

The deadlines are four independently chosen numbers that agree by arithmetic coincidence, not by derivation. Which one is the source of truth? This page lays out the mechanism as it stands; the answer was decided 2026-09-14 — see § 8 and the decision record.

The two wedge conditions, and why they are two

Both derive their deadline from state the app already declared; nothing global is assumed. They stay two named conditions rather than one merged predicate, because the dump reports which fired and neither alone covers both shapes (observer_core.h:110-150).

The subtlety that cost a healthy node a reboot

§3.2 judges progress, not depth. It compares drained against the last value it saw, and only starts the clock when the mailbox is non-empty and the counter has not moved. The original version sampled mailbox depth at an instant, which aliases against the workload — and rebooted a node that was still draining and still printing ticks (#269). pending == 1 means nothing; pulse_drained() moving is the discriminator.

§ 4

comms — the wire

The wire has no timers of its own. It has sizes, and the sizes are why the node's reply budget is the number it is.

BoundValueWhy it constrains a timer
batch512 BA push is 5–6 6LoWPAN fragments and the datagram survives only if every fragment does. That is what a 2000 ms reply budget is waiting through.
value256 BRadio constraint, not preference — 6LoWPAN fragments above ~102 B.
legs per check-in2A pull and a push, each node-initiated, each idempotent, each with its own full reply budget. Both stamp nodes.last_seen_ms.
The consequence worth staring at

Two legs × 2000 ms = 4000 ms of reply budget against a 2000 ms cadence. A check-in whose replies are slow overruns its own period — and measured under a silent uplink the node delivers a 4.007 s effective tick while every deadline above it is still computed from the declared 2000. That measurement is the H1 evidence in §6.

§ 5

porta — the gateway

porta has exactly one liveness timer, and its decision rule has no constants in it at all — every number in the verdict came from the node.

TimerValueSourceWhat it guards against
SweepTick 30 000 ms sweep.go:20 Unbounded CPU spent re-forming verdicts. Its own comment names the cost honestly: it is a floor on detection latency — a node whose budget is shorter than one tick is detected at the next tick, not at its budget.

The rule, and the four verdicts it can reach

now − last_seen > bud, where bud is the budget the node declared. Order matters as much as the test does:

VerdictReached whenThe design point
neverSeenno exchange has ever arrivedJudgeable immediately and without a budget — "has this node ever spoken" needs no number (D5).

livesince ≤ budTested before the deafness check, because speech is positive evidence and outranks the gateway's own absence.

silentpast budget, and porta was listening throughoutThe only verdict that asserts something went wrong.
§ 6

custos — no liveness timer at all

Worth stating as a finding rather than an omission. custos holds exactly one timer, a 3000 ms graceful-shutdown deadline (custos.go:102), and it is not part of this path.

That is correct and should stay that way: custos is a view. It serves /dev/status and proxies /health and /manifest. A timer here would be a second opinion about liveness formed by something that holds no evidence — a fourth number to reconcile, guarding nothing.

§ 7

What derives from what

Only three real dependencies exist. Everything else in the tables above is independent, which is itself the finding.

  1. budget ← cadence × 8, at PortaBridgeNodeC6.nsl:100. Written by the node, about itself.
  2. wedge deadline ← the app's own period × 8, at observer_core.h:43. Computed in C, on the node, from state the app declared.
  3. porta's bud ← the node's published budget, read from obs/liveness. porta contributes no number of its own to the verdict.
And the one that is NOT a dependency

SweepTick = 30 000 is related to nothing above it. It is not derived from the cadence, not from the budget, and not from any measurement of the mesh. It is a constant on the other side of the wire that silently becomes the effective deadline whenever it exceeds the declared budget — which, at the shipping cadence, it always does: 30 000 against 16 000.

The two × 8 multipliers are also not a dependency on each other. They were chosen separately — one as #215's measured slack, one as #231 D2's promise — and they agree by coincidence. Change the cadence and both move together; change either multiplier and they silently diverge with nothing to catch it.

§ 8

The fork — #273, DECIDED 2026-09-14

Decided — H1, and the conflation underneath it

The answer is H1: the node's declaration is the source of truth and porta contributes no number to the verdict. But the walkthrough found the fork rested on a conflation — cad was doing two unrelated jobs — so the decision splits it into two declared numbers, appCad (node-side, never on the wire) and telCad (published, with a tolerated-miss count from which porta derives the budget). Decided, not built. The record is docs/design/2026-09-14-liveness-two-cadences-design.md; the sections below describe the system as it is today, which is what this page is for.

The fork as it stood is drawn in the ladder diagram, and its caption put it in one sentence:

"The node declares 2 s and 16 s. porta cannot form a verdict faster than 30 s, so bud is capped by a constant on the other side of the wire (H2). Under a silent uplink the node delivers 4.007 s while every deadline above is still derived from the declared 2 s (H1)."

  • H1 — the declared cadence is the truth. Everything derives from what the node says about itself. Then the node's measured 4.007 s under a silent uplink means the declared 2000 is already wrong, and the deadlines above it inherit the error.
  • H2 — porta's clock is the truth. Then bud is effectively floored at 30 000 whatever the node declares, and the node's carefully-chosen 16 000 is decoration on the gateway side — still real for the node's own watchdog, but not for the verdict. Rejected: a gateway-side constant deciding the deadline is the defect #273 names, so H2 cannot be the cure for it. What porta does instead is refuse — where the declared budget is shorter than one sweep, the verdict is #unknown naming all three numbers, rather than a silent 30 s cap.

H1 is the decision, and it unblocks #272's age rule in verdictFor. Two things the decision adds that neither H1 nor H2 contained, because the fork as drawn could not see them: the app-liveness deadline is inferred today (shortest declared period × 8) and that inference is wrong in both directions — too short for a fast-poll app, and absent for a run-once node, which declares no repeating timer and is therefore judged by nothing at all.

The gates that hold this behaviour

  • liveness-heartbeat — the detector sees a node with no app timer. Needs the Thread CONF_EXTRA: its claim is that nothing speaks between wedge and reboot, which is vacuous on a node that cannot check in at all.
  • liveness-noradio — #274's regression gate. The same node with no radio config: the bind fails and a deadline still exists. The red it is shaped to catch is silence, which is what made the defect invisible.
  • porta-silent — a node whose uplink is gone survives it. The gate that caught #269's false trip, which it was not built for.
