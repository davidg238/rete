# Line-in over the console cable — spike for rete#311

**Question (David, 2026-10-02):** the Toit and st-oevm generations mixed console and commands on one serial link
with SLIP. Is that needed again to get to one USB cable, on all boards?

**Answer: no.** Plain text lines sent down the console cable reach the VM on all three board families, while the
console keeps printing on the same cable. No framing was needed. What the boards did need is two different receive
paths, and the C6 one was found by hanging the board.

## What was built

- `platform_debug_readline` on Zephyr (`nodus/oevm/src/backend/zephyr/platform_zephyr.c`) — it was a stub answering
  "no line". It is the seam the desktop debugger already reads its `dbg:*` lines through (primitive `PRIM_pf_readLine`).
- `nodus/oevm/zephyr/ReadLineProbe.nsl` — prints `rl:ready`, then echoes each line as `rl:got <n> <line>`.
- `nodus/oevm/zephyr/prj_linein_spike.conf` — the one Kconfig line the C6 path needs.

## Result per board

| board | console | receive path | lines echoed | longest line | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RP2350-Zero | USB-CDC, the only cable | polled, armed once | 6 of 6 | 200 chars whole | not measured |
| `nrf4ec0` (dongle) | USB-CDC, the only cable | polled, armed once | 5 of 5 | 200 chars whole | static RAM unchanged (257948 B); flash not isolated from the probe's own size |
| `c6spare` | CP2102N UART bridge | **interrupt-driven**, 256-byte queue | 27 of 27 | 400 chars whole; 20 lines back to back, 20 of 20 | not measured |

All sends were made through a second handle on the port while the console collector kept reading it (RP2350: one
handle, no collector).

## Two findings

1. **A USB-CDC console takes no input from a pure poller.** The class driver starts its first OUT transfer only from
   `irq_rx_enable` (or from a `poll_in` that already got a byte), so the desktop's write times out
   (`SerialTimeoutException: Write timeout`, `nrf4ec0`, 13:52). One `uart_irq_rx_enable` call fixes it; `poll_in`
   then works. Same code in both Zephyr trees (`usbd_cdc_acm.c`).
2. **A polled reader hangs the C6.** Once about `CONFIG_UART_ESP32_RX_FIFO_THRESH` (22) bytes queue in the UART
   FIFO the whole system stops, and the hardware watchdog resets it ~22 s later
   (`rst:0x7 (TG0_WDT_HPSYS)`, three times: 13:58:46, 13:59:34, 14:00:17). Lines up to 24 chars always worked; a
   50- or 200-char line always hung it; a 30-char line did both. The same 200-char lines sent in 8-byte chunks 20 ms
   apart went through 5 of 5 — so it is the queue depth, not the length. **The cause is not established**: the
   build has no UART interrupt handler, and what stops the system was not traced. The interrupt-driven path does not
   show it (400 chars, and 680 bytes back to back).

## Part 2 — the debugger on the C6 (same day, 15:50–16:10 MST)

**Question (David, the follow-up on rete#311):** the per-bytecode hooks, the enable flag, and a held program that
has timers. **Answer:** set a breakpoint, stop, step, inspect, continue — all on `c6bench` over its one cable from
the gateway; the board was held 21 s at the breakpoint against an 8 s liveness deadline and was not rebooted;
the debug build costs 11.6% per bytecode and 494 B of flash.

### What was built

- **The enable follows the build** (`platform_debug_enabled`, `platform_backend.h`). A board has no environment
  to carry `OEVM_DEBUG`, so the hooks image (`OEVM_DEBUG_HOOKS=1`) is the debug image: the Supervisor prints
  `dbg:ready` at boot and waits for the cable; the production image, hooks compiled out, never enters the loop.
  One switch, not two. Both image paths read it (`oimg_loader.c`, the solidifier's emitted entry); posix still
  reads `OEVM_DEBUG`, and the `debugger-integration` gate passes on both paths unchanged.
- **A held VM is not a wedged VM** (`observer_core.h` §3.2, `held`). The Zephyr read sets `debug_held` around
  its block; the observer samples it and re-latches the feed age while it is set, so the clock restarts from the
  RELEASE. §3.1 (a repeating timer whose ISR stopped) is deliberately not held off. Property 10 in
  `tests/test_observer_core.c`: held 80 s past an 8 s appCad, no trip; released and still stuck, fires 8 s after
  the release. (Before the change the same timeline trips at 9 s: the property fails to compile without the
  field, and fails at the first held sample with it absent from the rule.)
- `zephyr/DebugProbeC6.nsl` — a 1 s timer, `appLiveness: 8000`, each tick calls `countTo:` (the host gate's shape).
- `zephyr/tools/dbg_session.py` — ONE handle on the cable; `session` runs the whole flow and prints a verdict,
  `run` just sends the boot-time `dbg:continue` and streams.

### What this does not cover

- `dbg:pause` does not exist: a running program cannot be stopped from the cable; breakpoints are set at boot
  (`initialLoop`) or while paused. The boot-time wait has no timeout, deliberately — a debug image is on a cable by
  definition; what would earn a timeout is wanting a hooks image to run unattended, which nothing does today.
- `CONFIG_UART_INTERRUPT_DRIVEN` is still a spike overlay (`prj_linein_spike.conf`), not in `prj_esp32c6.conf`:
  folding it in changes every C6 image including the bell's, which this session did not flash.
- Breakpoints are still `<methodId> <offset>`; no front end from a file and line. The `.stmap` beside the image is
  the other half.
- **Coincident, not proven:** `c6bench` logged `rst:0x1 (POWERON)` at 15:49:16, minutes after a pyserial open on
  its CP2102N with DTR and RTS deasserted, while the collector held the port. Opening a second handle may reset a
  C6 through the devkit's auto-reset pair; the session driver pulses RTS on purpose, so it did not care.

## Part 3 — `dbg:pause`, one loop, and an afternoon lost to the driver (same day, 19:20–20:40 MST)

**Question (the queue on rete#311):** stop a RUNNING program from the cable, so breakpoints are not boot-time only.
**Answer:** a line arriving while the program runs stops it at its next bytecode; on `c6bench` the stop landed
within one tick, the loop answered, and the ticks resumed — 17 of 17 steps, no reset. The boot wait became the
first pause, so there is one command loop instead of two.

### What was built

- **The rule** (`platform_backend.h`, `platform_debug_pending`): the backend's receive path raises a flag when a
  newline lands while the VM is not held in `platform_debug_readline` — the UART ISR on the C6, SIGIO on stdin on
  posix — and the interpreter's single-step hook reads it beside `g_single_step` on every bytecode (one load, debug
  build only). It fires the same `onBreak:` hook a step does; with no step mode and no breakpoint record to match,
  the Supervisor announces `dbg:paused pause <methodId> <offset>` and the loop's first line is the one that
  arrived. So `dbg:pause` is only the verb a person types (answered `dbg:ok pause`); `dbg:break 3 0` sent while
  running works the same way. Lines that arrive while the VM is held are not counted — the loop is already reading
  them in order — which is what lets a driver pipe a whole script.
- **One loop.** `run:` arms one step before launching the app; the first pause prints `dbg:ready`, and
  `initialLoop` is deleted. Every verb works at boot. EOF in the loop clears stepping, since the boot stop and a
  pause arrive as a step.
- **`dbg:inspect` cannot kill the program.** A pause lands anywhere; registers hold blocks, contexts and vtables,
  which answer no `printString` (on the host the first pause aborted the VM with a DNU). `describe:` tells them
  apart by vtable identity and prints `[a block]` etc.
- **`CONFIG_UART_INTERRUPT_DRIVEN` is in `prj_esp32c6.conf`**; `prj_linein_spike.conf` is deleted. Every C6 image
  carries it from the next flash — the bell's included, which was not flashed today.
- Gate: `tools/test_debugger_integration.py` grew a pexpect case against `47_debug_pause_target.nsl` (a 200 ms
  wall-clock ticker; the pause must land before `tick 2`). `dbg_session.py session` grew the pause steps.

| **pause** | `dbg:pause` at 20:54:49.892, tick 32 just printed | `dbg:paused pause -1 2` at 20:54:50.695, then `dbg:ok pause` | 0.8 s: the VM was asleep in the pump and stopped at the first bytecode of tick 33 |

### What this does not cover

- A program with no timer and no work never reaches a bytecode and cannot be paused. The polled USB-CDC console
  (dongle, RP2350) has no asynchronous receive path: a line there waits for the next hold. A line sent during a
  long `dbg:over`/`dbg:out` lands when the step does.
- The file:line front end is still the other half (the `.stmap`).

### The driver, not the board (19:36–20:33)

The first session passed 16 of 17: `dbg:ok pause` was not seen for 5 s, then arrived together with the answer to
the next command. Six builds chased it on the board — uptime-stamped `printk` at readline entry and exit, a
transmit-idle spin before the block, a 5 ms busy-wait, a once-a-second heartbeat from the observer thread — and
each said the VM had answered on time while the host saw the answer only when it next sent a line. The cause was
`dbg_session.py`'s own reader: a generator that read a chunk, split lines and yielded them was abandoned by
`expect()` at its match, leaving the next line whole in its buffer; the next `expect()` split only after a fresh
read returned data, so a response printed 2 ms after the previous line (same chunk) surfaced only when more bytes
came. Fixed by draining already-whole lines before any read. The breakpoint path never showed it because each of
its answers arrived in its own chunk. Rule kept in memory: when a device "answers only when poked", read the host's
reader first.

**Found during:** every pyserial open of the CP2102N port rebooted the C6 (`rst:0x1 (POWERON)`), with DTR and RTS
both deasserted at open — ten times today, 100%. That is rete#319's question, answered for this devkit and this
adapter; it is why `dbg_session.py` runs one session per open and why a collector must be stopped first.

## Part 4 — by source line, for a person (same day, 22:00–22:40 MST)

**Question (FOCUS, the last queued item on rete#311):** file:line → `dbg:break <id> <off>`
through the `.stmap` and the registry, and a page for a human. **Answer:**
`nodus/nsl/tools/dbg.py` and `docs/tutorials/04-debugger.md`; on `c6bench` (the part 3
image, `ga65380e-dirty-20261002T205341`, unchanged) a breakpoint by line, `where`, `list`,
`inspect`, two line-steps, `clear`, a pause of the running ticker and the resume, all as
source lines, no reset (`~/rete-bench/311-fe/dbg-fe-session2.log` on the gateway).

- The `.stmap` now names its `entry` class: the join of stmap (class>>selector → lines)
  and `dbg:methods` (id → selector) needs it. The board run used the 20:53:33 stmap of
  the flashed build with that one field added by hand, since its methods are unchanged.
- A line step is bytecode steps until the line changes. Over the cable each step is a
  round trip of about 0.2 s; the first `next` (line 54 → 55, `n` = 1) took 3.6 s.
- **Found (session 1, `dbg-fe-session1.log`):** `finish` out of `main:args:` (a loop that
  never returns) left the VM stepping for good. Ticks ran 70 s, the armed breakpoint in
  `countTo:` never fired, and `pause` was never answered. `onBreakContext:`'s step path
  decides by depth alone and never reads the breakpoint table, so `next` over a call
  passes breakpoints too (rete#321). The same session also raced: a piped `list` right after
  `continue` stopped the program before the breakpoint (the pause rule, working).
